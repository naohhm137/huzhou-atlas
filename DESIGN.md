# Visual direction

The site is a dark, photographic atlas titled “层见湖州”. It opens on the real waterway of Nanxun. Fine ink green surfaces, pale paper text, and restrained brass highlights hold the film and models together. Chinese serif display type carries the story; a purpose-loaded Chinese sans face handles navigation, controls and longer explanations. Each chapter gives the exhibit a large uninterrupted stage and keeps explanatory text brief. The opening title no longer enlarges full-size commas and periods.

Navigation is an edge aligned wordmark with direct chapter links. The footer closes as a small source colophon. The primary visual rhythm is moving image, quiet 3D exhibition, moving image, and another exhibition.

Motion is explanatory. Video follows scroll position without an exposure jump; the poster uses the actual first film frame. Blender-authored model parts move from stored original transforms, allowing accurate reverse travel. The full-screen exploded mode arranges the Feiying Pagoda's seven outer timber-and-brick floors, seven roofs and five-layer stone inner tower as separate readable systems. The tea mode separates tray, cup, a shallow amber liquid lens and seven tea-bud groups, so the liquor no longer reads as a second ceramic cup. A gently inclined brush display makes orbit rotation legible; pointer drag rotates and wheel input zooms only when expanded, leaving normal scroll navigation intact. Blender-generated PBR textures and a restrained studio environment supply material grain and glaze reflections. Related, credited photographs sit behind each exhibit, filtered to retain foreground legibility. Controls use short transitions. Reduced motion keeps the content and direct interaction while removing scroll driven camera travel.

The added city half inherits the same paper/ink/brass world but changes scale rather than inventing four more museum objects. A typographic pause carries the visitor out of the gallery. Yishang Street fills the frame with a night street and a bridge viewpoint; Dongwu's actual twin-tower photographs rise through a restrained vertical reveal; Longemont's International Circus appears through an arch-like iris, keeping its vivid color as the section's exception; Nanxun closes with a keyboard- and pointer-operable two-angle river-lane comparison. This is photographic interpretation, not architectural reconstruction or a before/after claim. Each scene has a distinct scroll-linked property driven by the same reversible story conductor. At 601–900px, exhibit photo captions leave the stage to prevent collision with the chapter labels; all image authors remain credited in the footer.
